# What loadpath could not represent (and what we did about it)

The drone is a full-stack test of loadpath. Every place where the library could not model the real
physics is written down here, with what was fixed in loadpath and what is still open.

| # | Gap | Why it matters for the drone | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inertia relief balanced loads with the part's own (design-space) mass only | Motors, battery and stack are ~80% of the drone's mass; a thrust case pulled on the frame's corners instead of on the battery in its middle (a dumbbell bar read half its bending moment) | **Fixed** in loadpath 3b58223: `inertiaRelief.masses` |
| 2 | Natural frequencies only for a clamped part | A drone in flight is free-free; its first flexible mode is what the props excite | **Fixed** 3b58223: free-free modes for inertia-relief parts, carried masses included |
| 3 | A carried mass could only sit on its mounts | The battery's centre is ~23 mm above its posts: in a crash its inertia twists the posts (m a h), and it rocks on them at a far lower frequency | **Fixed** a0e14d3: `masses[].at` (+ `size` / `inertia`) makes it a rigid body |
| 4 | Cooling had only still air (h = 10 W/m^2K) outside | In flight the canopy sits in an 8 m/s stream: h ~ 51 | **Fixed** e244720: `cooling.skin.airflow` (flat plate + natural + radiation) |
| 5 | One safety factor for every load case | Flight loads want SF 2 plus fatigue; a crash may use most of the strength (SF 1.25) | **Fixed** f18c53d: `loadCases[].safetyFactor` |
| 6 | No aerodynamic cost for structure in a rotor's wake | Arms and spokes under the prop block thrust; the optimizer sees them as free | Measured 44121c7 (`aero.rotor_blockage`); not yet an optimizer term |
| 7 | Vibration is a floor (`minFrequency`) only | The 1x rotor line sweeps 179-497 Hz and blade pass 536-1490 Hz with throttle; a floor above the 1x band is the best available | Open (floor at 1.1 x full-throttle 1x = 546 Hz) |
| 8 | No ducted-propeller thrust model (fan.py is a fan against a system curve) | Duct inlet lip and tip gap set the thrust gain | **Fixed** loadpath eb7ed92: `aero.duct_performance` (hum duct: 1.125x the open rotor at equal induced power, lip costs most) |
| 9 | Cooling airflow is steady; a cycle cannot change it per phase | The worst case is idling on the ground with the VTX on (no flow), then flight cools it | Open |
| 10 | Motor heat softening the PA12 under the motor | Motors run 60-80 C; PA12 loses stiffness well before that | Open (durability at 50 C only) |
| 11 | The impact model crushes flat layers along one axis; a round tyre meets a wall at a point | The cap's first layers are tiny, so even at the softest printable density the force ramps up: the 4 m/s hit reads 499 N against the 395 N the energy alone would allow in 10 mm. A tyre profile shaped for constant layer area (flatter faces) would do better | Open (frame designed for the 499 N the bumper gives) |
| 12 | The impact model takes one material limit per study; SLS TPU's 1.0 mm walls cannot make a gyroid soft enough | Forced FDM TPU 95A (0.45 mm lines, 7 mm cells) for the bumpers | Noted |
| 13 | A free part's islands and hold | The coarse start had 88 cut-off islands and the 3-2-1 hold sat on corners the optimizer empties: the solve never converged | **Fixed** fd490f9: largest body kept, hold on drawn geometry |
| 14 | Drawn walls thinner than a cell broke into edge-hinged pieces | The 1.2 mm duct between the prop's air and the bumper became 96 pieces joined only along edges: a mechanism, solve stalled at 2100 iterations | **Fixed** a6e19ca: preserved cells joined through faces |
| 15 | Crash forces as static loads on a rigid frame | The bumper's force was applied as if the frame did not move; a ring that gives takes most of the energy at far lower force | **Fixed** loadpath 3e6f4f5: impact cases with bumper and frame in series. With it, 2 m / 4 m/s still read SF 0.2-0.8 on a 164 g frame, so the rating is 1 m / 3 m/s |
| 16 | Modes not in the optimizer for a free part | The canopy and battery rocking on their posts sat at 153 Hz, inside the rotor band | **Fixed** loadpath aa97546 |
| 17 | No propeller design: the prop's thrust was a test-stand number (610 gf) and its blade wasn't designed at all | Thrust per watt at hover is the drone's flight time; a printed blade flexes, and the full-throttle thrust is where the motor's torque meets the blade's on a sagging pack | **Fixed** loadpath 1f0b44d: `loadpath.propeller` (blade-element momentum on the printed section's own polars, motor and pack, the flexing blade, the design). Within 14% RMS of published printed-prop tests |
| 18 | A printed blade untwists at speed | The centrifugal twisting moment turns a PA12 blade about 8 deg flatter at the tip at 30k rpm, which loses about a quarter of its rigid thrust. Its lag and flap modes can sit on 2/rev at full throttle | Modelled (in 17); designs carry it. No thrust-stand check yet |
| 19 | Full-throttle thrust was a habit (T/W) | The mission sets it: hold height at a 60 deg bank carrying the 100 g the gripper picks up | spec.py `required_full_thrust` (2.04 N per rotor) |
| 20 | Prop roots were joined to the hub with a sharp corner, and the hub was as tall as the root wanted (22 mm) | A blade meets its hub through a fillet sized from the root's stress concentration, and the hub is only as tall as the motor's shaft takes (6 mm), so the root necks to a shank that fits. A 3D solid check of the spinning blade shows the fillet takes the junction's stress from 8.2 to 2.4 MPa. Fitting the hub cost 20-50% of hover power on PA12, and the soft shank untwists 15-25 deg at full throttle | loadpath propeller.root_fillet, design(hub_height=), solid_check; src/props.py |
| 21 | The beam's root stress reads 5-6x the solid's on the ducted 2-blade (25 vs 3.9 MPa) | Open: find which load or section property the strip beam overstates at a necked root, since that beam is what the optimizer's safety-factor limit runs on | loadpath propeller.structure |
| 22 | A stiff PA12 ducted frame can't take the crash rating at any sensible mass | Ducted frame optimized for flight plus 1 m drop and 3 m/s wall: 110 g at 30% volume (SF 0.21), 148 g at 60% (SF 0.38; flight cases SF 10). The 10.5 mm TPU tyres already on the ducts plateau at about 740 N in the 1 m drop; bringing that to what a PA12 ring holds would take about 25 mm of crush. PA12's strength per gram is the limit. Next (Connor's call): off-the-shelf carbon tubes as the core with printed nodes and ducts, a lower rating, or breakaway ducts | runs/frame1 (loadpath run, 2026-11-12) |
| 23 | A carbon-tube core doesn't rescue the ducted frame's crash rating | Four 8 mm pultruded tubes on the diagonals, bonded: 150 g at SF 0.49 against 148 g at 0.38 all-PA12. The tubes hold (SF 2.7, shear) and the bonds just about do (1.4-3.6); the printed duct ring fails locally where the tyre bears (drop-cg 140 MPa on the duct wall). The ring itself has to strain without breaking, which is what a TPU or PP duct does on a bought cinewhoop | runs/frame-carbon (2026-11-13), studies/frame-carbon.study.json |
